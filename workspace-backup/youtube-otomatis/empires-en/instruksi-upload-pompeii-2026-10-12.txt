In a single morning, 20,000 people died where they stood — no war, no plague. The mountain did it. This is Pompeii, the city time forgot to destroy, walked street by street.

I am Professor Adrian, and what amazes me about Pompeii is not the destruction. It is how ordinary it all was: bread in the ovens, election posters on the walls, a dog chained at its post. Pliny the Younger watched the eruption from across the bay and wrote it down letter by letter — we still read his account today. In 1863 Giuseppe Fiorelli poured plaster into the ash and gave the dead back their faces. And in 2018, a single charcoal inscription quietly moved the date of the disaster itself.

Pompeii is not just a tragedy about a volcano. It is a mirror — and a warning, because Vesuvius is still breathing above three million people.
