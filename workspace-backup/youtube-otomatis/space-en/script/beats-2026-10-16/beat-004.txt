The Great Attractor sits about two hundred and fifty million light years away. That sounds far, but in cosmic terms it is practically our next door neighbor. Here is the problem. It hides behind the brightest, dustiest part of our own galaxy. Astronomers call that band the Zone of Avoidance, because it avoids being seen. Two hundred and fifty million years ago, when the light we see today began its journey, dinosaurs had not even appeared on Earth yet.
