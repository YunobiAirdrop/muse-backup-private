HIROSHIMA 1945: THE DAY THE WORLD CHANGED FOREVER

[beat-001] At eight fifteen on a quiet summer morning, the sky above Hiroshima flashed brighter than a thousand suns. No warning. No thunder. Only light, where a living city had been.
[beat-002] August sixth, nineteen forty-five. A Monday morning. Children walked to school. Streetcars crossed the bridges. Office workers arrived at their desks. The city woke like any other day.
[beat-003] High above, a single American plane crossed the pale blue sky. Nobody looked up. Why would they? Just one plane, on one quiet morning. There was nothing at all to fear.
[beat-004] Then came the flash. Silent and blinding. [JEDA] In a single second, a city of three hundred thousand souls vanished into light, and history broke in two. Nothing would ever be the same.
[beat-005] I keep coming back to one question. How did humanity reach the point of splitting the atom to destroy a city? To answer, we must rewind the clock. Wait. It started like this.
[beat-006] In December nineteen forty-one, Japan attacked Pearl Harbor. Overnight, America was dragged into a war it had tried for years to avoid. The Pacific would burn for years.
[beat-007] What followed was four years of island hell. Iwo Jima. Okinawa. Japan's soldiers refused to surrender, and every single battle became a bloodbath without mercy. The fighting grew more savage each month.
[beat-008] Okinawa alone claimed two hundred thousand lives. Every island was a fortress. Every cave, a grave. The war was devouring a generation, and there was no end in sight.
[beat-009] Imagine being there. Nineteen years old, packed shoulder to shoulder into a landing craft, knowing the next beach would be worse than the last one. The ocean stretched on forever.
[beat-010] American planners did the math. Invading Japan's home islands could cost a million casualties. American and Japanese alike. Nobody wanted that terrible math. The numbers were simply unthinkable.
[beat-011] Something had to end this war before the invasion began. [JEDA] And in the quiet deserts of New Mexico, that something was already being built in total secrecy.
[beat-012] In nineteen forty-two, America gathered the greatest scientific minds on earth. One secret mission. Build the most powerful weapon the world had ever seen. The stakes could not have been higher.
[beat-013] The project was so secret that even the Vice President knew nothing about it. Two billion dollars spent. Entire hidden cities built almost overnight. No one was allowed to ask questions.
[beat-014] In the New Mexico desert they built Los Alamos. A town that appeared on no map, guarded by fences, secrets, barbed wire, and silence. Science and secrecy lived side by side.
[beat-015] Albert Einstein had warned the President first. The atom held unthinkable power. And everyone in Washington believed Germany was racing to reach it first. Time was running out fast.
[beat-016] What strikes me most about this is the terrible paradox. The finest minds of the age, working day and night, building the age's greatest horror. Genius had built a monster.
[beat-017] On July sixteenth, nineteen forty-five, they finally tested it. The Trinity test. A fireball rose over the desert, brighter than the dawn itself. The desert had witnessed the future.
[beat-018] President Truman learned of the bomb while he was in Germany. In his hands now sat a weapon that could end the entire war in a single day.
[beat-019] He was told Japan would fight to the last man, woman, and child. A blockade, his advisers said, would instead starve millions slowly and cruelly. Every option meant terrible suffering.
[beat-020] The decision was made. A list of target cities was drawn up. Hiroshima. A busy port. A military hub. Until now, strangely untouched by American bombs. One of them would be chosen.
[beat-021] On August sixth, Colonel Paul Tibbets lifted off from the island of Tinian. His bomber had a name. Enola Gay. He had named it for his mother.
[beat-022] The bomb had a name too. Little Boy. Four tons of uranium and steel. [JEDA] A four thousand pound secret riding in the belly of a lone bomber.
[beat-023] At eight twelve, the bomb was armed and the plane steadied on its run. The target: a bridge in the beating heart of Hiroshima. Three minutes to go.
[beat-024] Bomb away. The Enola Gay banked hard and fled for its life. Forty-three seconds later, at five hundred eighty meters above the city, Little Boy exploded. The countdown had reached zero.
[beat-025] The flash. The fireball. A mushroom cloud climbing nine miles into the sky. [JEDA] And below, a living, breathing city became silence. The silence said everything.
[beat-026] The heat melted stone and steel. The blast flattened every building within two miles. Rivers boiled. That is as much as we will ever describe. Some truths are too dark to show.
[beat-027] By the end of the year, one hundred forty thousand people were gone. Most of them civilians. Most of them gone in an instant, before they even knew what happened.
[beat-028] Three days later, a second bomb fell on Nagasaki. Even then, Japan's leaders hesitated and argued. Then, on August fifteenth, the Emperor did the unthinkable. The end was finally coming.
[beat-029] For the first time in history, Japan heard its Emperor's voice on the radio. Calm. Ancient. [JEDA] The war was over. Peace had a voice at last.
[beat-030] Hiroshima became a warning to the whole world. A city rebuilt from its ashes, now preaching peace to a world that kept building more and more bombs.
[beat-031] The man who led the project, Robert Oppenheimer, would later remember the ancient words of the Gita. I am become Death, the destroyer of worlds. He carried those words forever.
[beat-032] The atomic age had begun. Every war since has been fought in the shadow of that flash, and every leader since has lived with the same terrible button.
[beat-033] Imagine being there. Standing in the Peace Memorial Park today. The dome still stands among the trees. Ruined. Kept that way, deliberately, as a warning. The past refuses to disappear.
[beat-034] I have told many stories here. But this one stays with me. [JEDA] In a single morning, humanity learned how to destroy itself forever. We must never forget it.
