beat-001: On Thanksgiving Eve, nineteen seventy one, one man pulled off the perfect crime — and vanished forever.
beat-002: He hijacked a Boeing seven two seven, took two hundred thousand dollars, and jumped into the night.
beat-003: Nobody ever found him. Nobody learned his name. This is the legend of D B Cooper.
beat-004: Some say he died that night in the storm. Others say he lived quietly for decades afterward.
beat-005: The F B I chased him for forty five years — but never solved aviation's greatest mystery.
beat-006: Tonight we follow the evidence: the note, the money, the parachutes, and the ghost.
beat-007: November twenty fourth, nineteen seventy one. Portland, Oregon. A man buys a one way ticket to Seattle.
beat-008: He is calm, polite, middle aged. He wears a dark suit, a white shirt, and sunglasses.
beat-009: The ticket agent reads the name: Dan Cooper. It will become the most famous name never solved.
beat-010: Flight three oh five is a Boeing seven two seven, a short hop north, barely thirty minutes.
beat-011: Cooper boards with a briefcase and a calm smile, taking seat eighteen E at the very back.
beat-012: Six crew members. Thirty six passengers. Nobody knows the man in the last row has a plan.
beat-013: He orders a bourbon and soda. He lights a cigarette. The flight leaves Portland right on schedule.
beat-014: Soon after takeoff, Cooper hands flight attendant Florence Schaffner a folded envelope. She expects a phone number.
beat-015: Instead she finds a note, in calm block letters: I have a bomb in my briefcase.
beat-016: Thinking it is a prank, she asks for proof. Cooper opens the briefcase just enough.
beat-017: Inside: red sticks, wires, and a battery. It looks like a real bomb. Her blood runs cold.
beat-018: Schaffner carries the note to the cockpit. Captain William A Scott reads it in silence.
beat-019: The captain orders her to stay in the cockpit. Tina Mucklow will sit beside the hijacker instead.
beat-020: Cooper is polite to everyone, pays for his drinks, and keeps his cigarette lit. He is ready.
beat-021: His demands are clear: two hundred thousand dollars in cash, four parachutes, and a refueled airplane.
beat-022: He wants the money in a knapsack, in negotiable American currency, and parachutes ready when they land.
beat-023: Seattle air traffic control hears the news. The F B I mobilizes. Everyone is told: cooperate fully.
beat-024: Airline president Donald Nyrop authorizes the ransom payment. No one is to risk the passengers.
beat-025: For two hours, Flight three oh five circles over Puget Sound, waiting while the money is gathered.
beat-026: A local bank produces ten thousand twenty dollar bills. Agents record every single serial number.
beat-027: A skydiving school donates four parachutes. Two front, two back, just as Cooper ordered.
beat-028: At five forty five in the evening, long after sunset, the plane finally lands in Seattle.
beat-029: An isolated spot on the tarmac. Floodlights. Snipers. The F B I watches the airplane's every movement.
beat-030: Cooper demands the passengers stay seated. Tina Mucklow carries the money and parachutes aboard alone.
beat-031: The exchange begins. Thirty six passengers walk free, filing past the hijacker one by one.
beat-032: Cooper keeps the two pilots, the flight engineer, and Tina Mucklow. Everyone else is gone.
beat-033: He inspects the money, then the parachutes, checking every strap and cord like an expert.
beat-034: He orders a new flight plan: Mexico City, with a fuel stop in Reno. Low and slow.
beat-035: The landing gear stays down. The flaps set at fifteen degrees. Slow speed, low altitude.
beat-036: At seven forty five, Flight three oh five lifts off again into the black rainy night.
beat-037: Behind the plane, two Air Force fighter jets follow unseen, tracking the hijacker's every move.
beat-038: Cooper orders the crew into the cockpit, locks the cabin door, and stays alone with the money.
beat-039: He ties the cash to his body, puts on the parachutes, and faces the brutal storm outside.
beat-040: The rear of the seven two seven hides a stairway that opens mid flight. Cooper knows it.
beat-041: He lowers the stairs into the darkness. The wind roars. Rain and freezing air flood the cabin.
beat-042: Around eight eleven, the crew feels a sudden bump — the stairs jerking upward, then settling.
beat-043: Cooper is gone. Somewhere over the dark forests of southwestern Washington, he has stepped into the void.
beat-044: He jumps from ten thousand feet into rain, wind, and pitch black forest, wearing a business suit.
beat-045: The fighter jets see nothing. In Reno, at ten fifteen, the crew finds an empty silent cabin.
beat-046: Eighteen days of searching follow. Helicopters, soldiers, and agents comb the cold wilderness. Nothing.
beat-047: No parachute. No money. No body. The man who robbed the sky has simply ceased to exist.
beat-048: The F B I launches the largest manhunt of its era, questioning hundreds of suspects across America.
beat-049: A reporter misprints Dan Cooper as D B Cooper — and the legend gets its immortal title.
beat-050: Copycats try the trick again. The F A A orders locks fitted to every seven two seven.
beat-051: They call it the Cooper Vane — a small device locking the stairs shut in mid flight.
beat-052: Years pass. Theories bloom. Was he a paratrooper? A pilot? A desperate man with nothing to lose?
beat-053: In February of nineteen eighty, a family on a picnic finds something that changes everything.
beat-054: On the Columbia River shore, three rotting bundles of twenty dollar bills: five thousand eight hundred dollars.
beat-055: The serial numbers match the ransom money. It is Cooper's cash, lost to the river for years.
beat-056: But the money raises new questions. How did it get there? Did Cooper drown in the Columbia?
beat-057: Search teams return to Tina Bar. They dig, they sift, they scan. No more money is found.
beat-058: The remaining one hundred ninety four thousand dollars is never recovered. Not one bill.
beat-059: He left behind two parachutes and his black tie — clues that never led to a name.
beat-060: Cigarette butts were saved — but in nineteen seventy one, no D N A testing existed.
beat-061: The F B I built a profile: ex military, experienced parachutist, calm under pressure. A ghost.
beat-062: For decades, amateur sleuths studied the case. Every clue ended in the same dark forest.
beat-063: One suspect was Richard McCoy, who hijacked another seven two seven just five months later.
beat-064: McCoy demanded half a million dollars and parachuted out. But he was caught within days.
beat-065: Was McCoy also Cooper? Investigators studied the cases. The timelines never quite fit together.

beat-068: In twenty sixteen, after forty five years, the F B I quietly closed the case for good.
beat-069: They said they would reopen it only if the parachutes or the money ever surfaced.
beat-070: But the legend only grew. Books, films, festivals — Cooper became an American folk hero.
beat-071: Some admire the daring. Others remember he threatened innocent lives. The debate never ends.
