Now — the smoking gun. The city of Ugarit, on the coast of modern Syria, was one of the richest trading ports of the age. Around eleven ninety BC, it was destroyed so completely it was never rebuilt. But the archaeologists found something extraordinary: the city's archives — clay tablets — still sitting in the kilns where scribes had left them. And the last letters are terrifying. The final king, Ammurapi, writes that enemy ships are approaching, and begs a neighboring ruler for help. Another letter says the city's troops and chariots are away in Lukka, and the grain ships never arrived.  And here is the detail that gives me chills: some tablets were found UNFIRED — the scribes were still writing when the city started burning. Think about that for a second. The last words of a civilization, baked into clay by the very fire that destroyed it.
