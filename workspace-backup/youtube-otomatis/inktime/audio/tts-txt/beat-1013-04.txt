So who attacked? Egypt gives us a name — carved in stone. The pharaoh Ramesses the Third, around eleven seventy-seven BC, left a giant inscription at his temple at Medinet Habu. It describes a terrifying confederation arriving from the sea. His words: "The foreign countries made a conspiracy in their islands... No land could stand before their arms." Egypt called them the Sea Peoples — the Peleset, the Shekelesh, the Sherden, the Denyen. Ramesses claims he stopped them in two epic battles: one on land, one at sea — the first naval battle in recorded history. Egypt survived. But here is the problem: nobody knows where the Sea Peoples came from. Were they invaders? Refugees fleeing some OTHER disaster? Pirates? Historians have argued about this for a hundred years. The Sea Peoples might not be the cause of the collapse at all — they might be its VICTIMS.
