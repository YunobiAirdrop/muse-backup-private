So why did they work so little? One word: holidays. And I do not mean beach vacations — I mean HOLY days. The medieval church calendar was absolutely packed. Besides the fifty-two Sundays, there were roughly eighty feast days a year when work was forbidden — saints' days, Easter week, the Christmas season, harvest festivals. Skip church or work on a feast day, and you could face punishment or a fine. So add it up: fifty-two Sundays plus eighty holy days — that is around one hundred and thirty days off a year. One hundred and thirty. More than a third of the year... without working. Think about that the next time you have to beg for two weeks of vacation. But here is the question nobody asks: if they worked so little... why were they so poor?
