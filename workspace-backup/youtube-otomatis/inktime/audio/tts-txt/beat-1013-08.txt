But here is the twist nobody expects: from the ashes came the world we live in. Greece entered a dark age that lasted centuries — but Egypt survived, wounded, never again the superpower it was. And the real winners? The survivors on the margins. The Phoenicians — master sailors from the Levantine coast — built a new trading world. And something even bigger changed: iron. Bronze needed tin from far away. Iron ore? It is practically everywhere. Iron tools and weapons were cheaper, and anyone could make them. Robert Drews and other historians argue this democratized warfare — and power — forever. The Bronze Age elite needed a global trade network to keep their bronze monopoly. Iron broke the monopoly. The collapse did not just destroy a world — it accidentally invented a new one.
