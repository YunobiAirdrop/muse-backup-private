To understand how shocking this was, you have to see what existed BEFORE. Around twelve hundred BC, the ancient world was not primitive at all. It was the closest humanity had ever come to a global civilization. Mycenaean Greece. The Hittite Empire in Anatolia. New Kingdom Egypt — the Egypt of the pharaohs at their peak. And wealthy merchant cities like Ugarit on the Syrian coast. These kingdoms wrote letters to each other. We have them — the Amarna Letters — where great kings address each other as "my brother." They traded copper from Cyprus, tin from as far away as Afghanistan, grain, glass, gold. Tin plus copper makes bronze — and bronze was the steel of its day: tools, weapons, everything. One historian, Eric Cline, calls it the first international world system. And then — within a single lifetime — almost all of it was gone.
