But here is the part of this story nobody romanticizes: peasant life was BRUTAL. Yes — fewer total hours. But those hours were spent breaking their bodies. No sick days. No weekends like ours. And harvest season meant dawn-to-dusk labor, sometimes sixteen hours a day under a burning sun — because if the grain rotted in the field, people starved. The historian John Hatcher wrote about harvest workers laboring with terrifying intensity, knowing winter was watching. And the payoff for all those days off? Most peasants were dead by their mid-forties. Childbirth, infections, a bad tooth — things we fix with a single pill — killed them. What fascinates me about this is the trade-off: they had TIME, but they had no safety. We have safety... but we have no time. Neither deal is great.
