And that is where the mystery gets deeper — because the newest research says there was no single cause. Eric Cline, in his famous book "Eleven Seventy-Seven BC: The Year Civilization Collapsed," argues for what he calls a "perfect storm" — a systems collapse. Listen to how many disasters hit AT ONCE. Climate scientists found evidence of the worst drought in three thousand years across the eastern Mediterranean — crops failed, famine spread. There was a storm of earthquakes — one study counts a whole series of quakes shaking city after city. The military historian Robert Drews argued that warfare itself changed: cheap infantry with javelins could now defeat the elite chariot armies the palaces depended on. And the trade network? Tin came from thousands of miles away. When the routes broke down, there was no tin — and without tin, no bronze. No bronze, no tools. No weapons. The whole economy was built on bronze, and bronze simply... ran out.
