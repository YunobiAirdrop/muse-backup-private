For over a thousand years, nobody could explain it. Was it a plague from the gods? A bad omen? A dying star? The answer was buried — quite literally — under two miles of ice in Antarctica. In twenty fifteen, a team led by Michael Sigl drilled into Greenland and Antarctic ice cores and found something staggering: a massive spike of volcanic sulfate, trapped in ice layers dated to exactly five thirty-six. That sulfate could only come from one thing: a volcanic eruption so enormous it blasted sulfur into the stratosphere, where it spread around the entire planet and dimmed the sun. The mystery had a culprit. A volcano had turned off the sky.
