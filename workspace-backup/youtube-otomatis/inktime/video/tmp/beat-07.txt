Because five years after the darkness, the plague arrived. In five forty-one, ships docking at the Egyptian port of Pelusium carried something far deadlier than grain: the bacterium Yersinia pestis. The Plague of Justinian — named for the emperor Justinian the First — would kill an estimated twenty-five to fifty million people. Up to half the population of the Eastern Roman Empire. Now, was the plague caused by the eruptions? Not directly. But scientists now think the volcanic winter pushed rodents and fleas into closer contact with humans, weakened immune systems through famine, and destabilized trade routes — creating the perfect conditions for a pandemic. The volcano lit the fuse. The plague was the explosion.
