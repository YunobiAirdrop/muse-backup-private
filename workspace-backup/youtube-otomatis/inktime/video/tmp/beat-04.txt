And then trees told the same story. Dendrochronologists — scientists who read the history of the world in tree rings — looked at ancient oaks in Ireland, pines in Scandinavia, and bristlecone pines in California. And in all of them, the rings from the year five thirty-six are some of the narrowest in two thousand years. A study published in twenty oh eight found that the summers of five thirty-six to five forty-five were among the coldest of the last two millennia. Trees barely grew. Imagine being a farmer and watching your harvest die two summers in a row — with no idea that a volcano half a world away was to blame.
