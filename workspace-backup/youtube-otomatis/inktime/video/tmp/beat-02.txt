Our eyewitness is Procopius of Caesarea — a Byzantine historian, writing in Constantinople in the year five thirty-six. He reports that during this whole year, a most dread portent took place. The sun's light grew dim. People could stare straight at it, he says, because it shone without its usual heat. And he wasn't alone. Half a world away, a Roman statesman named Cassiodorus wrote that the sun appeared blue, and that the shadows had vanished from the earth. In China, chroniclers recorded snow falling in July and August. In Ireland, monks noted that bread failed — there was no grain. Think about that for a second. From Constantinople to China to Ireland, the same impossible sky.
