The sun went out. Not metaphorically — literally. For eighteen months, the sky turned a sickly blue-gray, crops withered in the fields, snow fell in the middle of summer, and one of the greatest historians of the ancient world wrote, terrified, that the sun gave forth its light without brightness, like the moon. And nobody knew why. This is the story of the year five thirty-six — a year one historian calls the worst year to be alive in all of human history. And it starts with a mystery scientists only solved about a decade ago.
