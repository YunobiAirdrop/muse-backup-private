And here is where the sky's failure becomes a human catastrophe. Cassiodorus, writing to his fellow Romans, warned them to stockpile grain — because the summer had been winter, and the harvest had failed. Prices exploded. Famine swept the Mediterranean, the Middle East, and Asia. Byzantine tax records collapse. Irish annals record failed harvests for years. In Mesopotamia, snow fell in a land of deserts. What fascinates me about this is how fragile everything was — and honestly, still is. One volcano, one dimmed sun, and entire civilizations went hungry.
