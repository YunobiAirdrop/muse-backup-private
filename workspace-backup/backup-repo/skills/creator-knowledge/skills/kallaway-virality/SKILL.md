# Virality — Kallaway Method

## Core Philosophy

Virality is engineered, not lucky. The formula is built on **audience matching** — making content for the same specific person repeatedly so the algorithm can lock into your slice and push further each time.

Two types of distribution exist:
- **Human-to-human:** Manual shares via DM or text.
- **Algo-to-human:** Platform pushes your content to strangers unprompted.

The goal is triggering algo-to-human distribution on demand. Algorithms are ad businesses optimizing for **user minutes** = (users on platform) x (minutes per session). Two metrics drive user minutes:
1. **Shares** — bring new users to the platform.
2. **Average Viewer Duration (AVD)** — keep existing users watching longer.

Chase these two. Everything else is a proxy.

**Do not chase pure virality.** The Viral Trap: big view numbers from wrong audiences = wrong algorithm signals, wrong followers, low conversion. The correct goal is **on-target virality** — the biggest ball that still fits all the way down your target buyer's slice of the algorithm wheel.

> "Ideas are the single biggest differentiator between winners and losers on social media." — Kallaway (citing Mr. Beast)

## Key Frameworks

### 1. The Virality Formula — 3 Variables

Before scripting anything, gut-check these three:

**Variable 1: Idea — Common vs. Uncommon**
- Common idea = something anyone with a pulse recognizes ("Bitcoin just crashed")
- Uncommon idea = weird, novel, edge-case that most haven't seen
- Target: uncommon, OR common with an uncommon point of view

**Variable 2: Addressable Audience Size (TAM)**
- How many people would share this if it crossed their feed?
- An uncommon idea with a tiny audience won't travel — not enough people to pass it along
- Require an unnaturally large applicable audience

**Variable 3: Point of View / Story Lens**
- Uncommon idea + normal lens = viable
- Common idea + uncommon lens = viable
- Common idea + common lens = will not go viral

**Validation method:** Find if a badly-made version of your idea already got millions of views somewhere else. If it did, the idea is validated — you just need to execute better.

### 2. The 9 Content Attributes

**Bucket A — Getting Attention (drives shares)**

| Attribute | Description | Target Score |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| TAM Resonance | How many people find this interesting? Use cult hopping to expand TAM | Maximize |
| Idea Explosivity | Shock score = delta from baseline belief to new reality introduced | 80–90 / 100 |
| Emotional Magnitude | Strength of emotional response: shock > fear > anxiety > sadness > joy | 80–90 / 100 |
| Novelty | Are you first, or does your frame feel like first? | 8–10 / 10 |

**Bucket B — Holding Attention (drives AVD)**

| Attribute | Description | Benchmark |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| Speed to Value | Seconds until novel value is signaled | 7–10s (short), 45–60s (YouTube) |
| Curiosity Amplitude | Open loop tied directly to viewer's biggest pain point | High |
| Absorption Rate | % of words per second actually understood | Maximize via simple words + visuals |
| Rehook Rate | Second curiosity loop reopened mid-video | 20–25s (short), every 3–4 min (YouTube) |
| Stickiness | Is this the viewer's most important pain point? | Must be a top-3 pain point |

**Cult hopping:** Reference a famous celebrity, brand, or movement to expand TAM. Do not force it — it must naturally connect to the topic.

### 3. Content Legos — 7 Bricks

Every video = 7 independent bricks. Change one at a time to diagnose and fix underperformers.

| Lego | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Topic | One-sentence description of what the video is about |
| Angle | The premise, take, and key facts used to explain the topic |
| Hook Structure | Spoken + text + visual hook combined |
| Story Structure | How the story is delivered (breakdown, listicle, tutorial, etc.) |
| Visual Format | Layout (green screen, POV vlog, faceless, etc.) |
| Key Visuals | Actual A-roll, B-roll, animations shown on screen |
| Audio | Background music and sound effects |

For YouTube, add: Title + Thumbnail = 9 bricks total.

**Signature style principle:** Lock in hook format, storytelling style, visual format, and audio. Only swap topic, angle, and key visuals video to video.

### 4. Outlier Research System — 3 Steps

**Step 1: Find Outliers**
- Outlier score = video views / channel's average views
- A small channel with a 50x outlier is more signal-rich than a big channel with a 2x outlier
- Use Sandcastles.ai: filter outlier score 5x+, engagement rate 5%+, post date 7–14 days (trending) or 3–12 months (evergreen)

**Step 2: Analyze Across All 7 Lego Bricks**
- Study top 10 performers in your niche
- Identify patterns: what topic, hook structure, visual format, audio mood keeps recurring?
- These patterns = validated answers for individual bricks

**Step 3: Hold Winners, Remix the Rest**
- Keep the bricks the outlier did best; replace weak ones with your own versions
- Typical move: change angle, hook, visual format; keep topic, some key visuals, audio mood
- Apply the 3% Rule (Virgil Abloh): you only need to change 3% of something to make it your own

**4-Box Research Matrix — always search same content type first:**

| | Same Platform | Different Platform |
|---|---|---|
| Same Content Type | Reels on Instagram | Reels on TikTok/YouTube |
| Different Content Type | Static posts | Tweets, blogs |

Source ideas only from the top two boxes. Format-specific psychology transfers within the same content type.

### 5. Concentric Circles — 40-40-20 Rule

Four rings of content targeting from innermost to outermost:

| Ring | Name | Description | DeFi Example |
|------|------|-------------|--------------|
| 1 | Core | Target buyer's exact problem in their niche | DeFi yield strategies for LATAM |
| 2 | Inner | Target buyer's problem in adjacent niches | Crypto investing for beginners |
| 3 | Outer | Target buyer's problem in any niche | Anyone new to investing |
| 4 | Max | Broadest possible topic connection | Anything finance-related |

**Content mix for turning views into dollars:**
- 40% Core
- 40% Inner
- 20% Outer
- 0% Max

Never make Max content. It goes viral but attracts the wrong audience, breaks algorithm signals, and converts at the lowest rate.

---

### 6. The Viral Trap — Ball and Wheel

Social media = a wheel where each slice = a topic category. The algorithm swivels toward the right slice.

- Center of wheel = passive entertainment seekers, low buying intent
- Edge of wheel = specific pain points, high buying intent, buyers
- Pure viral = ball gets so big it can't travel past center
- On-target viral = biggest ball that still reaches the edge of your target slice

---

### 7. Content Diagnostic — 3-Stage Funnel

Diagnose underperforming videos in order. Never fix the bottom while the top is broken.

**Stage 1: The Click (Top of Funnel)**
- Matching Problem: wrong people being served → reset account signals, post 20 disciplined on-topic videos, comment in your niche
- Packaging Problem (YouTube): right people served but not clicking → title + thumbnail must trigger pain point + solve + proof

**Stage 2: The Opt-In (Middle of Funnel)**
- Idea Problem: not valuable enough → score novelty, applicability, uniqueness (1–10 each); weak one-liner = weak idea
- Hook Problem: idea is good but hook didn't grab → establish context + agitate pain point, then contrast embedded belief with something opposite
- Comprehension Problem: viewer confused → simpler words, shorter sentences, active voice
- Boredom Problem: speed to value too slow → cut fluff, speak faster, harsher cuts, more visual switching

**Stage 3: The Share (Bottom of Funnel)**
- Takeaway Problem: no clear payoff → rework back half so tactical nugget or emotional payoff is explicit
- Uniqueness Problem: lacks signature wow factor → more reps (this develops over hundreds of videos)

## Process

### Step 1 — Validate the Idea

Run the Virality Formula Gut-Check:
1. Is the idea uncommon? Or do I have an uncommon angle on a common idea?
2. Is the addressable audience unnaturally large?
3. Does a badly-made version of this already exist with millions of views?
4. Does this fall in Core, Inner, or Outer ring (never Max)?

### Step 2 — Score the Idea

Assess across three dimensions (1–10 each):
- **Novelty:** How new or novel is this? Are you among the first?
- **Applicability:** Can the viewer immediately use this to solve their problem?
- **Uniqueness:** Is the packaging different from everyone else covering this topic?

A weak score on any dimension = rework before scripting.

### Step 3 — Score the Shock and Emotion

- Set baseline viewer expectation for the topic
- Measure the delta between baseline and what your content reveals
- Assign shock score (1–100). Target: 80–90.
- Assign emotional magnitude score (1–100). Target: 80–90.
- Identify the primary share emotion: shock, fear, anxiety, sadness, happiness, joy

### Step 4 — Write and Check the Script

Use the Short Form Script Review Checklist:
- [ ] Value signal within first 7–10 seconds?
- [ ] First sentence makes viewer need the second sentence?
- [ ] Sentence lengths vary (jagged, not rectangular)?
- [ ] 3–4 word jabs scattered throughout for rhythm?
- [ ] "But" or "however" used 2–3 times as rehooks?
- [ ] Clickbait phrases embedded (celebrity names, "world's first," contrarian claims)?
- [ ] Complex concepts simplified to pairworthy visuals?
- [ ] Final line is a punchy question, joke, or cultural reference?

Run the Commentary Value Score (CVS) Test: predict the top three comments — one from a superfan, one from a neutral observer, one from a hater. If you can't identify a clear diving board into discussion for all three, the script needs a stronger point of view.

### Step 5 — Run the 4 On-Target Virality Filters

1. Idea falls in Core / Inner / Outer ring (not Max)?
2. Finished video delivers walk-away value?
3. Would one on-target viewer share this with another on-target viewer (peer-to-peer within niche)?
4. Same audience profile as my other successful videos?

## Anti-Patterns

**Chasing pure virality.** Big numbers from wrong audiences build the wrong algorithm signals and convert near zero. Resist vanity metrics.

**Making Max content.** Broadest possible topic = most views = worst business outcome. Stick to 40-40-20.

**Inventing ideas from scratch.** Validated outliers exist. Human psychology is consistent. Use the 3% Rule — change just enough to make it yours.

**Changing everything when a video underperforms.** Changing topic, hook, format, audio, and visuals simultaneously makes it impossible to isolate the problem. Change one Lego brick at a time.

**Algorithm superstition.** The algorithm has no feelings. It pushes content that drives shares and AVD. That's it. If you get those metrics, it pushes you.

**Ignoring the Ideal Viewer Avatar.** Making content for multiple audiences scatters algorithm signals. Define one specific person and target them exclusively.

**Equal effort across the whole video.** The hook (first 5–30 seconds) deserves 30–50% of scripting effort. Everything after a retention drop-off is a black screen.

**Optimizing likes and follows.** Metric priority: Shares > Comments > Saves > Likes > Views. Shares are the only metric that directly drives new user acquisition on the platform.

### CVS Test — DeFi LATAM Content

Predict three comments before posting:

If you cannot predict all three, the content needs a stronger point of view or more specific claim.
