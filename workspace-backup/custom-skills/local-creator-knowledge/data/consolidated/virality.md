## Core Philosophy

### Virality Can Be Engineered
Kallaway's foundational position: virality is not luck. There is a formula. The formula is based around **audience matching** — making videos for the same type of person over and over. The tighter the audience match, the more the algorithm pushes the content algorithmically. He calls this the "bullseye Target lock."

> "Virality can be engineered. It is solvable. There is a formula for making videos that consistently work at will."

### Two Types of Content Distribution
- **Human-to-human:** You share a video manually via DM or text.
- **Algo-to-human:** The platform (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube) pushes your video to strangers without you doing anything.

The "cheat code to content" is triggering algo-to-human distribution at will.

### On-Target Virality vs. Pure Virality
This is a central distinction. **Do not chase pure virality.** Pure virality (millions of views from all audiences) is the "Viral Trap." The right goal is **on-target virality** — the biggest ball that still fits all the way down the slice where your target buyers live.

> "It's not pure virality that you're after. What you're after is on-target virality."

Pure virality creates the wrong audience, incorrect algorithm signals, and low conversion. On-target virality drives buyers, builds correct audience match, and compounds over time.

### What Algorithms Actually Want
Algorithms are ad businesses. They want **user minutes** = (number of users on platform) x (minutes per session per user). Two metrics drive user minutes:
- **Shares** — bring new users onto the platform.
- **Average Viewer Duration (AVD)** — keeps existing users on longer.

Every algorithm decision is in service of maximizing user minutes. If your content drives both shares and AVD, the algorithm will push it to everyone.

### Content Is an Outliers Game
The best way to spend content analysis time is to reverse engineer why an outlier happened. Not every video needs to go viral, but when one does, study it intensely.

> "Content really is an outliers game and when you get one the best way to spend your content analysis time is to reverse engineer why the outlier happened."

### Ideas Are Everything
Kallaway cites Mr. Beast repeatedly: ideas are the single biggest differentiator between winners and losers on social media and YouTube.

> "I always used to listen to Mr. Beast talk about this... and I'd always roll my eyes. But I couldn't have been more wrong about this."

A great idea can be 100x more sharable than an average idea — the difference between 10,000 views and 10 million views.

## Frameworks

### 1. The Virality Formula — Three Core Variables

Before scripting or editing, gut-check three variables:

**Variable 1: The Idea — Common vs. Uncommon**
- **Common idea:** Something anyone with a pulse would recognize (e.g., "Apple just dropped iPhone 16").
- **Uncommon idea:** Something weird, unique, or on the edge that most people haven't come across.
- For virality, go **uncommon** if possible.

**Variable 2: Addressable Audience Size (TAM)**
- How many people would potentially share this if it crossed their feed?
- An uncommon idea with a tiny applicable audience won't go viral — not enough people to share it.
- You need an unnaturally large applicable audience.

**Variable 3: Point of View / Story Lens**
- Common idea + unique lens = viable viral formula.
- Uncommon idea + normal lens (just tell what happened) = viable viral formula.
- Common idea + common lens = not going viral.

> "You have to have cracked this three-piece variable set of the virality formula. You have to have an uncommon idea or a common idea with an uncommon point of view, and you need an unnaturally large applicable audience."

**Validation method:** Find if a similar video already exists on another platform. If a terrible, poorly-edited version of your idea got 12M views somewhere, the idea is validated.

### 2. The Nine Content Attributes (Getting and Holding Attention)

Every piece of content must perform across two buckets: **getting attention** and **holding attention**.

#### Bucket A — Getting Attention (Drives Shares)

**Attribute 1: TAM Resonance**
- TAM = Total Addressable Market — how many people would find this video interesting?
- Bigger TAM = more share potential = more algo push.
- Two tactics to expand TAM while staying in-niche:
  1. **Broaden the premise** — simpler, broader framing increases the surface area of understanding.
  2. **Cult hopping** — reference a famous celebrity, brand, or movement to expand resonance. Do not force it; it must naturally connect to the topic.

**Attribute 2: Idea Explosivity**
- How shocking or unusual is the idea or frame? Kallaway measures on a **shock score (1-100)**.
- Shock = difference between current belief and new reality introduced in the content.
- Bigger delta = greater shock = higher likelihood of sharing.
- People only share 1-3 pieces of content per day max; shock is the most visceral share emotion.

**Attribute 3: Emotional Magnitude**
- How large of an emotional response (scale 1-100) does the idea create?
- Most shareworthy emotions (in order): shock, fear, anxiety, sadness, happiness, joy.
- Four tactical levers to increase emotional magnitude:
  1. Visuals showing the desired emotion on the subject's face.
  2. Music and sound effects that vibe-match the desired emotion.
  3. Vocal tone and pacing matching the desired emotion.
  4. Trigger words — specific words that match the desired emotion (e.g., say "This is scary" to make viewers feel fear).

**Attribute 4: Novelty**
- Being first or different when covering a story.
- People won't share something they've seen before — they won't get social credit for it.
- If you're late on news-based content, add a novel frame or premise to make it feel like you're first.

#### Bucket B — Holding Attention (Drives AVD)

**Attribute 5: Speed to Value**
- How fast can you tease the incoming value of the video?
- Every additional second past the beginning = 5-10% viewer loss.
- Audit exercise: Read script line by line. After every line ask: "Is it clear what the value is going to be?" If not clear by sentence 2, cut the fluff.
- Benchmark for short form: value signal in first 7-10 seconds. For YouTube: 45-60 seconds.

**Attribute 6: Curiosity Amplitude**
- How curious can you make the viewer (scale 1-100)?
- Curiosity = an open loop/question in the viewer's brain they need to close.
- The closer the question is to a real pain point, the higher the amplitude and the longer they'll stay.
- Tactic: Directly implant a rhetorical question tied to their biggest pain point.

**Attribute 7: Absorption Rate**
- Percentage of words per second actually understood by the viewer.
- Tactics: simplest words possible, shortest phrasing possible, pair words with visuals.
- Cannot implant an idea in someone's mind unless they understand what you're saying.

**Attribute 8: Rehook Rate**
- A **rehook** is a second (or third) curiosity loop reopened mid-video.
- Short form: rehook around the 20-25 second mark.
- YouTube: rehook every 3-4 minutes.
- Method: Open initial curiosity loop → close or jump to new loop → repeat.
- Transitional words: "but," "however," "but the truth is the bigger problem is actually this."
- Analogy: hitting the booster in a video game before the car runs out of gas.

**Attribute 9: Stickiness**
- How deeply does the topic or premise actually matter for this particular viewer?
- Synonym: relevance.
- If you've nailed the viewer's biggest pain point, they'll watch the whole video. If they're not facing the problem you're discussing, they'll bounce.
- Solve by always targeting the most important pain points for the ideal viewer avatar.

### 3. Idea Legos (Content Legos)

Every video can be broken down into seven independent Lego bricks:

| Lego | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Topic** | One-sentence description of what the video is about |
| **Angle** | The premise, take, and key facts used to explain the topic |
| **Hook Structure** | Spoken hook + text hook + visual hook combined |
| **Story Structure** | How the story is delivered — breakdown, listicle, tutorial, etc. |
| **Visual Format** | How the video is laid out visually (green screen, POV vlog, faceless, etc.) |
| **Key Visuals** | The actual A-roll, B-roll, animations shown on screen |
| **Audio** | Background music and sound effects |

For YouTube, add: **Title** and **Thumbnail** (9 total Lego bricks).

**How to use Content Legos for iteration:**
- Identify which single Lego is the weakest in an underperforming video.
- Isolate that brick, find a validated version from a top performer in the niche, swap it in.
- Run the experiment 2-3 times to confirm the hypothesis.
- This is the scientific method applied to content improvement — change one variable at a time.

**Signature style principle:** Top creators keep the same hook format, storytelling style, visual format, and audio from video to video. They only change topic, angle, and key visuals. Lock in a repeatable stack.

### 4. The Outlier Research System (Idea Mining — 3 Steps)

**Step 1: Outlier Research**
- Find videos in your niche that have already outperformed.
- **Outlier score** = views of one video / average views of that channel.
  - Example: 100K views on a channel averaging 10K = outlier score of 10x.
- Outlier scoring allows apples-to-apples comparison across channels of different sizes.
- A small channel with a 50x outlier tells you something important happened there.

**Step 2: Analyze Outliers Across 7 Lego Bricks**
- Study the best-performing videos for patterns across all seven Lego bricks.
- Ask: What topic, hook structure, visual format, audio mood keeps recurring in the top 10 performers?
- These patterns = the data telling you what works for this niche.
- Common patterns = validated answers for individual Lego bricks.

**Step 3: Hold Winners, Remix the Rest**
- Identify which Lego bricks the outlier did best.
- Keep those constant; replace the weak ones with your own versions.
- Analogy: five-card poker — break the video into 7 cards, keep the best, reshuffle the rest.
- Typical Kallaway move: almost always changes the angle, hook, and visual format; usually keeps topic, some key visuals, and audio mood.

### 5. The 4-Box Outlier Research Matrix

When looking for outliers to mine, there are four places to search (using fitness/Instagram as an example):

| | Same Platform | Different Platform |
|---|---|---|
| **Same Content Type** | Fitness reels on Instagram | Fitness reels on TikTok, YouTube |
| **Different Content Type** | Fitness static posts on Instagram | Fitness tweets, blogs |

**Golden rule:** Always look in the **top two boxes** — same content type, either same or different platforms. Reason: humans cannot outrun their psychology. Vertical video hits people differently than other formats. Consumption patterns are format-specific, so insights transfer within the same content type.

If you source ideas from different content types (tweets, blogs), you can steal the topic and angle but must rebuild all other Lego bricks from scratch — extremely difficult without expertise.

### 6. The Viral Trap — Ball and Wheel Metaphor

Social media is visualized as a giant wheel where each slice = a topic category. The width of each slice maps to the size of the audience in that space.

- **The ball** = your video, placed in the center of the wheel.
- The algorithm swivels the wheel to aim the ball toward the right slice.
- The faster the algorithm can identify the slice, the further down the slice the ball travels.
- **Center of the wheel** = passive viewers, entertainment-seekers, low buying intent.
- **Edge of the wheel** = specific pain points, high buying intent, buyers.

**The viral trap:** As the ball gets bigger (more views), it can no longer fit down narrow slices. Big viral videos stay in the center. High views, low conversion.

**On-target virality:** The biggest ball that still fits all the way to the edge of your target slice.

### 7. Concentric Circles of Idea Targeting (40-40-20 Rule)

Four rings of content ideas from innermost to outermost:

| Ring | Name | Description | Example (restaurant marketing) |
|------|------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 (innermost) | **Core** | Target buyer's specific problem in their specific niche | Restaurant marketing tactics |
| 2 | **Inner** | Target buyer's problem in tangential niches | Local brick-and-mortar marketing |
| 3 | **Outer** | Target buyer's problem in any niche | Any business struggling with marketing |
| 4 (outermost) | **Max** | Broadest connective tissue of the topic | Anything marketing-related |

**Content mix for turning views into dollars: 40-40-20**
- 40% Core ideas
- 40% Inner ideas
- 20% Outer ideas (controlled chaos)
- 0% Max ideas

At 5 videos/week: 2 core, 2 inner, 1 outer, 0 max.

**Never make Max content.** Max content goes viral but attracts the wrong audience, builds incorrect algorithm signals, and converts at the lowest rate.

### 8. The Four On-Target Virality Filters

Run every content idea through these four filters:

**Filter 1: The Idea Filter**
Will this idea be applicable and interesting to people in your target slice? Use the concentric circles framework (40-40-20) to guide selection.

**Filter 2: The Value Filter**
Does the finished video deliver **walk-away value** — a tactical tip, idea, or framework the viewer can apply to their problem immediately after watching?

**Filter 3: The Peer Share Filter**
If one person in your target slice watches this, will they share it with another person in the same target slice (peer-to-peer sharing within the niche)? This creates an **on-target virality loop**. Off-target shares dilute algorithm signals.

**Filter 4: The Audience Match Consistency Filter**
Does this idea target the same audience profile as your other videos? Consistency builds "grooves" in the algorithm's memory, making it faster at finding the right slice. The faster the algorithm finds the slice, the further the ball travels.

### 9. The Content Diagnostic — Three-Stage Viewer Funnel

A structured process for diagnosing why a video isn't getting views. Three stages, eight specific blockers:

**Stage 1: The Click (Top of Funnel)**

*Problem 1: The Matching Problem*
- Videos are not being served to the right people (content-audience mismatch).
- Solve: Start a fresh account if the current one has wrong followers. Post 10-20 disciplined videos on the new topic. Comment on as many videos as possible from creators in your space to signal niche membership to the platform.

*Problem 2: The Packaging Problem (YouTube only)*
- Right people are being served but aren't clicking.
- Title + thumbnail must trigger three things: **pain point + solve + proof**.
- Direct triggers in the title; indirect triggers in the thumbnail.

**Stage 2: The Opt-In (Middle of Funnel) — Drives AVD**

*Problem 3: The Idea Problem*
- Idea is not valuable enough (not interesting, useful, or relevant).
- Assess across three dimensions: **Novelty** (1-10), **Applicability** (1-10), **Uniqueness** (1-10).
- Easiest solve: watch it back. Is it actually interesting to you? If the one-liner doesn't create magnetic pull, the idea is weak.

*Problem 4: The Hook Problem*
- Idea is compelling but hook didn't grab the viewer.
- Hook must induce a curiosity loop.
- Two-step hook process: (1) Establish context + agitate pain point. (2) Contrast the embedded belief with something opposite or contrarian.

*Problem 5: The Comprehension Problem*
- Viewer is getting confused and bouncing.
- Retention charts are essentially comprehension charts.
- Solve: simpler words, shorter sentences, active voice, punchy tone.

*Problem 6: The Boredom Problem*
- Speed to value or speed of delivery is too slow.
- Solve: cut fluff before the value, speak faster, harsher cuts between sentences, more visual switching.

**Stage 3: The Share (Bottom of Funnel)**

*Problem 7: The Takeaway Problem*
- No clear payoff. People share to get social/professional credit from the recipient; they need a clear takeaway to share.
- Solve: Rework back half of script so the tactical nugget or emotional payoff is explicit or strongly implied.

*Problem 8: The Uniqueness Problem*
- Everything else is solved but the video lacks a "wow factor" — the signature style or invisible sauce layer.
- Solve: more reps. Kallaway had 600K followers and hundreds of millions of views before his signature style was fully present.

**Priority rule:** Solve problems in order. Never optimize the bottom of the funnel if the top is broken. Click → Opt-In → Share.

### 10. The 3% Rule (Virgil Abloh)

Designer Virgil Abloh's rule: "All you have to do to make something your own is take something that already works, change it 3%, and then you've got something new."

Kallaway applies this to content ideation:
- You don't need original ideas — humans behave the same way over and over.
- Top creators literally repost the same exact videos every 4-6 months and they work again.
- You can remix 20, 30, even 50% of an idea and it will still work.
- The 3% rule gives you permission to build on validated winners rather than inventing from scratch.

---

### 11. The Ideal Viewer Avatar

The foundational audience concept. Before making any video, clearly define one specific person you're making content for.

- Define their pain points, likes, dislikes — imagine a real person.
- Making content for multiple audiences at once = eliminating any free algo boost.
- Every time you switch audiences, it's like restarting from scratch.
- Tighter avatar = tighter audience match = more algorithm push.

> "People aren't watching videos for you. They're watching videos for them."

Deep down, viewers don't care about your story, your struggle, or your characters. They care about accomplishing their goals. Content should shift from "I" to "you."

## Templates

### Script Review Checklist (Short Form Video)
Use this before finalizing any script:

- [ ] **Speed to value:** Highlight the first line where something novel is introduced. Is it within the first 7-10 seconds? If not, cut everything above it.
- [ ] **Curiosity Loop:** Does the first sentence make the viewer need to hear the second sentence?
- [ ] **Rhythm and sentence variability:** Look at the script shape — does it have jagged edges (varied sentence lengths) or a rectangle (uniform lengths)? Uniform = boring.
- [ ] **Tags:** Are there 3-4 word jabs scattered throughout to break monotony and add rhythm?
- [ ] **Rehooks:** Is "but" or "however" used 2-3 times to re-hook and re-hook and re-hook?
- [ ] **Clickbait phrases:** Are there specific words, names, or phrases embedded that will predictably drive comments (celebrity references, "world's first," contrarian claims, etc.)?
- [ ] **Simplification:** Is every complex concept translated into the simplest possible version? Can the visual pair with the simplified description?
- [ ] **Last dab:** Does the final line deliver a punchy question, joke, or cultural reference that people will want to react to and share?

### Commentary Value Score (CVS) Test
Ask yourself: "What are the top three comments I expect to see from this video?"
- One from a super fan
- One from a neutral observer
- One from a super hater

If you can't identify a clear "diving board" into discussion for all three groups, the script is not emotionally charged enough. Increase CVS by:
1. Taking a stronger point of view on one side of the issue.
2. Using words that lead viewers into the desired emotional charge ("this is scary").
3. Seeding discussion with a subtle reference to how the target group will be specifically affected.

### Idea Quality Assessment
Score each idea across three dimensions (1-10 each):
- **Novelty:** How new or novel is this? Are you one of the first?
- **Applicability:** Can someone actually use this to solve their problem?
- **Uniqueness:** Is the presentation packaged differently from everyone else?

### On-Target Virality Gut-Check
Before posting any video, run through these four filters:
1. Does this idea fall into the Core, Inner, or Outer ring (not Max)?
2. Does the finished video have walk-away value?
3. Would one on-target viewer share this with another on-target viewer?
4. Does this target the same audience profile as my other successful videos?

### Virality Formula Gut-Check (Short Form)
1. Is the idea common or uncommon? (Target: uncommon)
2. What is the addressable audience size? Is it unnaturally large?
3. If the idea is common, do I have an uncommon point of view on top of it?
4. Have I validated this idea by finding a similar video that already performed well?

### Nine Content Attributes Checklist
Run every content idea through:

**Getting Attention:**
- [ ] TAM Resonance — Is the applicable audience large? Have I used cult hopping if needed?
- [ ] Idea Explosivity — Shock score above 50? Is the delta from baseline expectation large?
- [ ] Emotional Magnitude — Will this drive shock, fear, anxiety, or sadness in most viewers?
- [ ] Novelty — Am I first, or do I have a frame that feels like first?

**Holding Attention:**
- [ ] Speed to Value — Is the value signaled within the first two sentences?
- [ ] Curiosity Amplitude — Is there a rhetorical question directly tied to the viewer's biggest pain point?
- [ ] Absorption Rate — Simple words, short sentences, paired with visuals?
- [ ] Rehook Rate — Is there a rehook at the 20-25 second mark (short form)?
- [ ] Stickiness — Is this the viewer's most important pain point?

## Anti-Patterns

### 1. Chasing Pure Virality
The most dangerous mistake for business owners using content. Big views from wrong audiences = wrong algorithm signals, wrong followers, diluted niche authority, and low conversion. The viral trap is seductive because the numbers look impressive while the business impact is close to zero.

### 2. Speed vs. Size Fallacy
Assuming more information = more value. Cramming 10 concepts into one video causes information overload — viewers absorb nothing and leave on a bad note. The brain learns in bursts. Optimize for the speed to lock in ONE core learning, not the quantity of information.

### 3. Inventing Original Ideas From Scratch
Treating idea originality as a requirement. This is "one of the reasons why making content feels so stressful as a beginner." Validated outliers exist. Human psychology is consistent. What worked before will work again. Use the 3% rule.

### 4. Algorithm Superstition
Believing the algorithm has a personal stake in your success or failure — that it shadowbans you, hates you, or plays favorites. The algorithm has no feelings. It mechanistically pushes content that drives user minutes (shares + AVD). If you get those metrics, it pushes you. Period.

### 5. Changing Everything Between Reps
When a video underperforms, changing the topic, the hook, the format, the audio, and the visuals all at once. This makes it impossible to isolate what the actual problem was. The Content Lego method: change one brick at a time, test, confirm the hypothesis, then move to the next brick.

### 6. Equal Time Distribution Across the Video
Spending the same amount of time scripting and polishing every section. The hook (first 5-30 seconds) should get 30-50% of the effort. Everything after a drop-off might as well be a black screen.

### 7. Ignoring the Ideal Viewer Avatar
Making content for multiple audiences simultaneously or making content without defining an avatar first. This scatters algorithm signals, prevents audience match from building, and makes every new video feel like starting over.

### 8. Making Max Content
Making broad, maximally viral content for views at the expense of niche targeting. Max content will generate the biggest view numbers but builds the wrong audience, dilutes the algorithm's ability to find your slice, and converts at the lowest rate.

### 9. Misreading Metrics
Optimizing for likes and follows instead of shares and AVD. The metric priority order:
1. Shares (most important)
2. Comments
3. Saves
4. Likes
5. Views

Likes signal consensus. Comments signal contrarian takes. Saves signal dense, informative content. Shares signal remarkable, emotion-driven content that induced a reaction strong enough to forward.

### 10. Thinking It's Too Late to Enter Content
"Every content niche is full" is a false limiting belief. Since for-you algorithms became dominant (around 2022), followers and subscribers are no longer gatekeepers to views. Anyone can get a million views on their first video. The quality bar is higher, but the playing field is more level than in any prior era.

## Real Examples

### The Iron Man Video — 46 Million Views Case Study
**The idea:** A Russian engineer building a real, functional Iron Man suit in his garage.

**Virality formula breakdown:**
- **Common or uncommon:** Uncommon — as uncommon as it gets.
- **Addressable audience:** Anyone who has seen the Iron Man movie (massive TAM) + anyone into robotics or engineering. Together: extremely large applicable audience.
- **Point of view:** Normal lens (just tell people exactly what happened) — the uncommon nature of the idea made a fancy lens unnecessary.
- **Validation:** Found a video about this on Twitter with 12 million views. Critically, the Twitter video was "terribly edited, no storytelling" — white space to improve on.

**Six script decisions:**
1. **Curiosity Loop** — "This guy's building a real Iron Man suit in his garage but to do it he had to invent the world's first personal hydrogen reactor."
2. **Rhythm and sentence variability** — Jagged edges on the script. Varied sentence lengths create unpredictability and better listening rhythm.
3. **Tags** — 3-4 word jabs between longer sentences ("It's pretty nuts").
4. **Rehooking** — "but" used three times: "but to do it," "but he also built this," "but the craziest part of all."
5. **Clickbait phrases embedded intentionally:**
   - "world's first personal hydrogen reactor" — predicted comments disputing it.
   - "Russian engineer" — intentionally placed to trigger US vs. Russia comments.
   - "the real Tony Stark" — celebrity hold for audience to grab onto.
   - "invent" — predicted disbelief comments.
   - "just like the one in the movie" — movie comparison gives audience a familiar hold.
6. **Last dab** — "he really did it in a cave with a box of scraps" — famous line from the movie, drove massive comment replication.

**Eight edit decisions:**
1. **Key visual** — Alex's YouTube channel had extensive footage of building the suit.
2. **Visual hook** — Iron Man helmet shown immediately, arrow pointing to the suit, text "Russian engineer builds Iron Man suit" on screen. "People see before they hear."
3. **Music** — ACDC/Black Sabbath-style Iron Man-themed track, volume dropped low with a low-pass filter (cuts high frequencies, preserves bass to not muddy the voice).
4. **Riser effects at rehooks** — Sound riser + rapid scale increase on the video frame at each "but" moment to create a physical jolt of attention.
5. **Arrows** — Used to direct viewer eyes to the key element being described (hydrogen reactor chest piece, etc.).
6. **Captions** — Two types: yellow Roka Heavy font captions + white bold text for emphasis moments. 50% of viewers watch without sound.
7. **Pacing** — Tight cuts, no dead space, 4-5 cuts within a sentence. Pacing calibrated to the BPM of the music.
8. **Loop** — The last frame shows the Iron Man helmet in the same position as the first frame, creating an accidental loop. Comments about the loop drove viral sharing.

**Results:** 46 million total views across platforms. 4 hours of work. 8 days after posting. 26K followers gained.

## Metrics & Benchmarks

### The Two Metrics That Matter Most
1. **Share rate** = shares / total views. Brings new users to the platform. Drives initial reach.
2. **Average Viewer Duration (AVD)** = average length of time viewers watch. Keeps existing users on the platform.

All other metrics are secondary. These two, multiplied together, = user minutes = the thing algorithms optimize for.

### Metric Priority Order (Full Ranking)
1. Shares — remarkable, emotion-driven, induced a reaction strong enough to forward.
2. Comments — usually a contrarian take; people come to argue.
3. Saves — dense, valuable, neutral-take content; worth bookmarking.
4. Likes — consensus take; people agreed.
5. Views — a lagging indicator of everything above.

### Speed-to-Value Benchmarks
- **Short form video:** Novel value signal must appear within the first 7-10 seconds.
- **YouTube long form:** Novel value signal must appear within the first 45-60 seconds.
- Every additional second = 5-10% of remaining viewers lost.
- Clear value signal by sentence 1: ideal. By sentence 2: acceptable. Sentence 3+: too slow.

### Rehook Timing
- **Short form:** Rehook at 20-25 second mark.
- **YouTube:** Rehook every 3-4 minutes.

### Outlier Score Thresholds (Sandcastles.ai filter settings)
- **Outlier score filter:** 5x and above.
- **Engagement rate filter:** 5% and above (screens out juiced brand deal posts).
- **Post date filter:** 7-14 days for news/trending content; 3-6 months or 12 months for evergreen ideas.
- Sort: outlier score descending.

### Content Mix Benchmarks
- **For business conversion (views to dollars):** 40% core + 40% inner + 20% outer + 0% max.
- **For pure media/audience building:** Mix shifts toward outer and max (pure virality acceptable).

### Commentary Value Score (CVS)
Not a numerical metric — qualitative. A video has high CVS if you can clearly predict three distinct types of comments: superfan, neutral, and hater. High CVS → algorithm push through comments and sharing.

### Shock Score
Kallaway's subjective 1-100 scale for idea explosivity:
- **1-15:** Too low. Not shareworthy. Nobody would think to share it.
