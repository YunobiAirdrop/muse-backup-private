# The Real Monetization Target

I checked YouTube's [official eligibility rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851) this morning, and I owe you a correction: the real bar for ad revenue is 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours in the last 12 months, or 1,000 subscribers plus 10 million Shorts views in the last 90 days. The "500 subscribers plus 3,000 hours or 3 million Shorts views" number I gave you yesterday comes from a smaller early-access tier, and it isn't the bar for ad revenue.

That changes the math I promised you yesterday. A single viral Short of 500 thousand to 1 million views covers five to ten percent of the Shorts bar, not the whole thing, so my claim that one hit could close the requirement was off by roughly ten times.

The two paths, side by side:

- Long-form channels: 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 qualified hours in 12 months. Shorts views don't count here.
- All-Shorts channels like @StrangeTales-g6n and @MacamMacamID: 1,000 subscribers + 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days.

The calendar shifts with it. Reviews take about a month, and a rejection means waiting 30 days to reapply, 90 if it keeps happening. So for the 10 January 2027 deadline, our first applications should go out in early December, not pile up in January. My new rule is simple: the moment a channel crosses the real bar, I file.

Here's what didn't change: the strategy is sound. YouTube's own [monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392) confirm it doesn't ban AI-assisted content; it bans inauthentic, mass-produced template content with no original perspective, and it explicitly allows the same intro and outro on every video as long as the main content differs. Our bumpers and the human-touch playbook are exactly what the rules ask for. One caution I'll hold myself to: on the body-language channel I'll keep the host voice as commentary and never claim expert credentials, because the rules bar AI personas on sensitive topics.
